## What it's about

The true Second World War story of British intelligence trying to fool the Nazis with a dead body and some very carefully planted papers. It's a real story with real stakes, and the show knows exactly how absurd it is, even Ian Fleming gets a solo.

Confession: I didn't know it was a comedy going in. The clips I'd seen on social media were of the one serious and gloomy song in the whole show, so I was expecting something sombre. I then spent two hours crying with laughter. I understand keeping the other songs under wraps, but I don't think the marketing does the show justice, as I would've come to see it WAY sooner if I had known its true nature.

## The humour

The comedy is physical, vocal and genuinely smart, and best of all, it's humour that doesn't punch down. It pokes fun at private schools and English government stereotypes, but all in good faith. There are little Easter eggs everywhere, and the fun starts before the show does: there was a quiz at our seats to match the ties to the private schools (I failed).

But it also knows when to stop joking. I cried both times I saw it. The switch between the silliness and the genuinely touching moments doesn't feel forced or take you out of it, if anything you appreciate the duality all the more.

## Standout performers

I saw Alex Young as Ewen Montagu and quite frankly, I fell in love. The charisma and personality she brought to Montagu were off the charts. One of the most outstanding performances I've ever seen, no wonder the internet is crushing on Montagu!

But I can't pick just one, because everyone was brilliant: Peter McGovern (Charles Cholmondeley), Roshani Abbey (Jean), Danny Becker (Hester) and Chlöe Hart (Johnny). On my first visit Geri Allen played Johnny, and on my second Madeleine Jackson-Smith played Jean.

The show is only five actors, with lots of multi-rolling and cross-gender casting, which keeps you on your toes, and suspending disbelief, takes you on a wild journey of plot discovery.

![Curtain call from the stalls, 21 June 2025](/reviews/operation-mincemeat/bows-stalls.jpg)

*A new cast took over in May 2026, so the company you'll see now is different from mine.*

## The music

I don't want to spoil it, as a lot of the story is told through the songs, but the cast albums are available on streaming platforms.

I will say this: Dear Bill. Tears, both times. Born to Lead is the opposite: pure fun, the best time.

I'll leave the rest for you to discover.

## Staging and design

On the surface the staging is simple, but the more you look, the cleverer it gets. The tables, the doors and the rest of the props are constantly moving and being reused: a table becomes a desk, then a board, and so on. It's all real and physical rather than projected (personally, not a fan of extensive use of projections), and it's all incredibly detailed. Take the map: from the stalls I didn't even notice it, but from the balcony on my second visit I could see just how detailed it is. The attention to detail is great, and I loved how every object gets a second (and third) life.

![Curtain call from the balcony, 4 November 2025: you can see the map on the set](/reviews/operation-mincemeat/bows-balcony.jpg)

The choreography is nothing too complicated, and quite frankly, it doesn't need to be.

## The venue

The Fortune is a small theatre, which works in the show's favour. I sat close in the stalls the first time and on the balcony the second, and both times the view was decent. No heads cut off, nothing important missed. The stalls are naturally better because you're closer, but in a theatre this size I don't think it matters much where you sit.

Seat tip: if the price is putting you off, enter the ticket lottery on the official website. The view from the lottery seats is still decent for what you pay, and it's a great way in if you're not ready to spend full price.

Also, keep an eye on the merch. They've created some very fun, witty and genuinely useful items, and the humour carries straight on from the show.

![Whole-Arsed Ideas notebook in the merch display, 4 November 2025](/reviews/operation-mincemeat/notebook.jpg)

## See it if you love...

- British humour
- A bit of Second World War history, especially if you know the real story
- Laughing until you cry

It's also one of the very few musicals I recommend to guys who aren't sure about musicals. I took one of my best friends, who has barely seen any (The Book of Mormon was the only other one he'd enjoyed), and he loved it.

I haven't seen the Broadway version, but from what I hear, US audiences love it too.

## Who might not enjoy it

Honestly, only people who are stuck up and can't take a joke. Pretty much everyone else will have a brilliant night.
